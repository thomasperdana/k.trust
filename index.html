Understanding the Purpose of a Trust

A trust is a powerful legal entity that acts as your instruction manual for wealth distribution and preservation

What is a Trust?

A trust is a legal entity that holds and manages assets on behalf of beneficiaries according to a set of instructions you define. It acts as an "instruction manual" for your assets after you pass away, ensuring your wishes are followed precisely.

Why Use a Trust?

Trusts provide multiple advantages including bypassing probate, controlling asset distribution, estate tax planning, and creating perpetuity for wealth preservation across generations.

Bypass Probate

Avoid the lengthy and costly probate process where courts and attorneys control asset distribution.

Saves 6-18 months & 5-10% in fees

Control Distribution

You decide how and when your assets are distributed, protecting your family from mismanagement.

Complete control over terms

Tax Planning

Reduce or avoid estate taxes that your heirs might otherwise have to pay on inherited assets.

Potential 40% tax savings

Key Components of a Trust-Based Family Banking System

Understanding the essential elements that make this system work

Ownership Structure

The trust owns the whole life insurance policies and is the beneficiary of the death benefits. This structure helps avoid probate and can reduce estate taxes if properly set up.

Trust Ownership 100%

Trustees and Rules

Trustees manage the trust and enforce rules such as loan approval processes, interest rates charged, and ensuring premium payments are always made to keep policies active.

  • Professional oversight
  • Clear loan guidelines
  • Interest rate policies
  • Regular compliance checks

Loan Management

Family members submit loan proposals reviewed by trustees. Loans are granted if the plan is sound, with failure to repay resulting in asset liquidation but not credit destruction.

Actionable Advice to Start Building Your Legacy

Step-by-step guide to implementing your family trust system

1

Educate Yourself and Your Family

Teach your children how money works and how to control it, including the concept of being the bank. Financial education is the foundation of lasting wealth.

Start financial education as early as age 6 with basic concepts
2

Consult Professionals

Work with experienced estate planning attorneys and CPAs who understand trusts and whole life insurance structures. Professional guidance is crucial.

3

Set Up a Trust

Decide on the type of trust (revocable, irrevocable, spendthrift, etc.) that fits your family's needs. The trust should own your whole life insurance policies.

4

Fund Whole Life Insurance Policies

Purchase whole life insurance policies owned by the trust on family members, especially children, to build cash value and death benefits over time.

5

Create Family Banking Rules

Establish clear rules for loan requests, interest rates, repayment, and use of funds to ensure responsible management and growth of the trust.

6

Use the Trust as Your Bank

Encourage family members to borrow from the trust for major expenses instead of traditional banks, keeping interest and payments within the family.

7

Maintain and Monitor

Regularly review the trust's performance, ensure premium payments are made, and adjust rules as needed to adapt to family growth and changes.
